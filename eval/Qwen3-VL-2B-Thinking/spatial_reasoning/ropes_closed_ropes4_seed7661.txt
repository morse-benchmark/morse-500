To determine how many **closed shapes** (i.e., **bounded regions**) are created by intersecting lines, we analyze the geometric configuration of the lines in the video.

---

### Step 1: Understand the Problem

The video shows **multiple lines** intersecting each other in such a way that **each pair of lines crosses once**, and **no three lines intersect at the same point**. This is a typical configuration for **general position** of lines in the plane.

The question asks:  
> **How many closed shapes do the intersecting lines create?**  
> *Answer with a single integer.*

This is a **classic combinatorial geometry problem**, where the number of **bounded regions** (closed shapes) formed by $ n $ lines in general position is given by the formula:

$$
\text{Bounded Regions} = \binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2}
$$

This formula counts the number of **triangles** or other bounded regions formed by the intersection of the lines.

---

### Step 2: Analyze the Video

From the video frames, we can observe:

- **Four lines** are drawn: yellow, red, green, and purple.
- Each line intersects every other line exactly once (no three lines meet at a single point).
- Therefore, $ n = 4 $.

---

### Step 3: Apply the Formula

Using the formula for the number of **bounded regions** formed by $ n $ lines in general position:

$$
\text{Bounded Regions} = \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4(4 - 1)}{2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6
$$

---

Each **bounded region** is a **closed shape** (a polygon or triangle) enclosed by the lines. For four lines in general position, **6 bounded regions** are formed.
