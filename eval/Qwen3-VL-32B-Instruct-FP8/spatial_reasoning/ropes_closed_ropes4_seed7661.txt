We are given a visual scene with intersecting lines, and we are asked to count how many **closed shapes** they create.

---

### Step 1: Understand the definition of a "closed shape"

A **closed shape** is a figure that is fully enclosed — meaning the lines form a continuous loop with no open ends. For example, a circle, triangle, or any polygon is a closed shape. In this context, we are looking for regions bounded entirely by the intersecting lines, where the boundary is made up of segments of the lines and forms a complete loop.

---

### Step 2: Analyze the visual (as described in the video)

From the video frames:

- At 0:04, a **yellow curved line** appears — it’s open, not closed.
- At 0:05, a **red line** appears, intersecting the yellow line.
- At 0:06, a **green line** appears, intersecting both yellow and red.
- At 0:07, a **purple line** appears, intersecting the others.

All lines appear to be **open curves** — none of them form a complete loop by themselves.

However, when they intersect, they may form **closed regions** — areas bounded by segments of different lines.

We need to carefully examine the intersections and identify how many **enclosed areas** are formed.
