We are given a video that shows a sequence of 3D cubes, each with colored faces. The question asks: **Which cube net folds into the cube shown in the video?** We are to choose from four options: A, B, C, D.

---

### Step 1: Observe the cube in the video

At 0:04, we see a row of 5 cubes. Let’s focus on the **first cube** (leftmost), since it’s the most clearly visible and likely the target.

The first cube shows:
- **Top face**: Red
- **Front face**: Green
- **Right face**: Blue

We can also see that the cube has other faces (back, left, bottom), but they are not visible. However, from the rotation at 0:06, we can confirm the color arrangement.

At 0:06, the cube rotates, and we see:
- The **red face** remains on top.
- The **green face** is now on the front.
- The **blue face** is now on the right.

This confirms the relative positions:
> Red is adjacent to both Green and Blue. Green and Blue are adjacent to each other (sharing an edge).

So, in the 3D cube:
- Red is on top.
- Green is front.
- Blue is right.

This means:
- Red is adjacent to Green (top-front edge)
- Red is adjacent to Blue (top-right edge)
- Green is adjacent to Blue (front-right edge)

So these three colors form a “corner” — all pairwise adjacent.

### Step 2: Examine the nets

We need to find which net, when folded, produces a cube where:
- Red, Green, and Blue meet at a common corner (i.e., are pairwise adjacent).

Let’s analyze each option.

---

#### Option A:

Layout:

```
   Red
White - Orange - Purple
   Green
```

Folding this:
- Red is on top.
- White is left.
- Orange is front.
- Purple is right.
- Green is bottom.

So, Red (top) is adjacent to White, Orange, Purple, and Green? No — in a cube, top is adjacent to front, back, left, right — **not** bottom.

So Red is adjacent to White (left), Orange (front), Purple (right), and **not** Green (bottom).

But in the target cube, Red is adjacent to Green (front) and Blue (right). Here, Red is adjacent to Orange (front) and Purple (right), and Green is opposite Red.

→ **Does not match**.

Actually, in Option D:

```

```

If we fold this so that **Red is on top**, how?

We can choose any face as top. The net is flexible — we can fold it so that any face becomes top.

So let’s try to fold Option D so that **Red is on top**.

If Red is top, then:
- The face to its right is Purple → that becomes front.
- The face to its left is... nothing? Wait, in the net, Red is connected to White (above), Purple (right), and Green (below? No — Green is below Blue).

Actually, let’s label positions clearly.

The net is:

Row 1: White (centered above Red)

Row 2: Red - Purple - Blue (horizontal)

Row 3: Green (below Blue)

So connections:
- White is attached to Red (above)
- Red is attached to Purple (right)
- Purple is attached to Blue (right)
- Blue is attached to Green (below)

So if we make **Red the top face**, then:
- White is the back face (since it’s attached to Red’s top edge — but in cube, top face’s “above” is not a face — we need to think in 3D).

Better approach: **Find which net has Red, Green, and Blue all pairwise adjacent.**

In Option D:

- Red is adjacent to White and Purple.
- Green is adjacent to Blue.
- Blue is adjacent to Purple and Green.
- Purple is adjacent to Red, Blue, and...?

So Red and Green are **not adjacent** in this net — they are separated by Purple and Blue.

Red is connected to Purple, Purple to Blue, Blue to Green — so Red and Green are two steps apart → **not adjacent**.

But in the target cube, Red and Green are adjacent (top and front).

→ So Option D is invalid.

Option C:

In target cube, Red is adjacent to Green and Blue — yes, here it is.

The key is: **Are Red, Green, and Blue pairwise adjacent?**

In Option C: Red adjacent to Green? Yes (top and right). Red adjacent to Blue? Yes (top and left). Green adjacent to Blue? Green is right, Blue is left — they are opposite, not adjacent.

In a cube, left and right are **opposite**, not adjacent.

So Green and Blue are **not adjacent** in Option C.

But in the target cube, Green (front) and Blue (right) **are adjacent** — they share an edge.

→ So Option C fails.

Option B:

If we fold so that **Red is top**, then:

- The face attached to Red is Green (above) — so Green becomes front? Wait.

Let’s try:

Then:

This is getting messy.

Better idea: **Look for the net where Red, Green, and Blue are all mutually adjacent.**

In a cube net, three faces that meet at a corner must be pairwise adjacent in the net — i.e., each pair must share an edge in the net.

So we need a net where Red, Green, and Blue are all connected to each other — forming a “corner” in the net.

Let’s check each option for that.

---

#### Option A:

Red connected to White and Orange.

Green connected to Orange.

Blue? Not in this net — wait, in Option A, the colors are: Red, White, Orange, Purple, Green — **no Blue**.

Wait a minute — in the video, the cube has **Blue** as the right face.

But in Option A, there is **no Blue** — it has Purple, Orange, etc.

Similarly, Option B has Blue, Green, Red, White, Purple — no Orange.

Option C has Red, Blue, Orange, Green, Purple — has Blue and Green.

Option D has White, Red, Purple, Blue, Green — has Blue and Green.

But in the target cube, the visible faces are Red (top), Green (front), Blue (right).

So the net must contain these three colors, and they must be pairwise adjacent.

Now, in **Option C**:

Red is adjacent to Blue and Orange.

Green is adjacent to Orange and Purple.

So Red and Green are both adjacent to Orange — so they are not directly adjacent.

In a cube, if two faces are both adjacent to a third, they may or may not be adjacent — depending on the net.

In this net, Red and Green are separated by Orange — so when folded, they might be opposite or adjacent.

Let’s simulate folding Option C:

Net:

```
   Red
Blue - Orange - Green
   Purple
```

Fold Blue up to be left face.

Fold Orange up to be front face.

Fold Green up to be right face.

Then Red is top.

Purple is bottom.

Now, Red (top) is adjacent to Blue (left), Orange (front), Green (right) — good.

Blue (left) and Green (right) are opposite — not adjacent.

But in target cube, Blue (right) and Green (front) are adjacent.

Here, Blue is left, Green is right — opposite.

So not adjacent.

→ Invalid.

- Red and Green are not adjacent — separated by Purple and Blue.

Option B:

Now, in the net:

- Blue and Green are adjacent.
- Green and Purple are adjacent.
- Purple and Red are adjacent.

So Red and Green are not directly adjacent — they are connected via Purple.

When folded, if Green is front, Blue is left, Purple is right, Red is bottom, White is top.

Then Red (bottom) is adjacent to Green (front)? Yes — in a cube, bottom is adjacent to front.

Similarly, Red (bottom) is adjacent to Blue (left)? Yes.

And Green (front) is adjacent to Blue (left)? Yes.

So in this folding:

- Red (bottom) adjacent to Green (front) and Blue (left).
- Green (front) adjacent to Blue (left).

So all three are pairwise adjacent — they meet at the bottom-front-left corner.

But in the target cube, Red is on **top**, not bottom.

However, we can rotate the cube — the relative positions are what matter.

In the target cube, Red is top, Green is front, Blue is right.

In this folding of Option B, we have Red bottom, Green front, Blue left.

But if we rotate the cube 180 degrees around the front-back axis, then:

- Bottom becomes top.
- Left becomes right.

So Red becomes top, Green remains front, Blue becomes right.

Perfect match!

So Option B, when folded with Green as front, Blue as left, Red as bottom, and then rotated, gives the target cube.

Therefore, **Option B is correct**.
