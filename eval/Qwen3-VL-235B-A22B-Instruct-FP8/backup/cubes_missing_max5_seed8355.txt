We are shown a 3D structure made of colored cubes, rotating, and then asked: “How many cubes are missing from this figure?”

This implies that the structure is meant to be a complete rectangular prism (or cube), and we must deduce the total number of cubes that *should* be there, subtract the number of cubes we can see, and find the missing ones.

---

### Step 1: Determine the dimensions of the complete structure

Looking at the rotating 3D structure, we can infer the bounding box — the full rectangular prism that would contain all cubes if none were missing.

From the various angles shown (especially 0:05, 0:07, 0:09, 0:10), we can see:

- The structure is **3 cubes wide** (x-direction)
- **3 cubes tall** (y-direction)
- **3 cubes deep** (z-direction)

This suggests the full structure is a **3x3x3 cube**, which would contain:

> 3 × 3 × 3 = **27 cubes**

---

### Step 2: Count the visible cubes

We need to count how many cubes are actually present in the structure.

Let’s go through the structure carefully.

We can break it down layer by layer (bottom to top).

#### Bottom layer (z = 1):

From the views, we can see:

- Front row: 3 cubes (left, center, right) — all present.
- Middle row: 2 cubes (left and center) — right is missing.
- Back row: 1 cube (left) — center and right are missing.

So bottom layer has: 3 + 2 + 1 = **6 cubes**

#### Middle layer (z = 2):

- Front row: 2 cubes (left and center) — right is missing.
- Middle row: 2 cubes (left and center) — right is missing.
- Back row: 1 cube (left) — center and right missing.

So middle layer: 2 + 2 + 1 = **5 cubes**

#### Top layer (z = 3):

- Front row: 1 cube (left) — center and right missing.
- Middle row: 1 cube (left) — center and right missing.
- Back row: 0 cubes — all missing.

So top layer: 1 + 1 + 0 = **2 cubes**

---

### Step 3: Total visible cubes

6 (bottom) + 5 (middle) + 2 (top) = **13 cubes**

---

### Step 4: Missing cubes

Total possible (3x3x3) = 27  
Visible = 13  
Missing = 27 - 13 = **14**

---

### Final Answer:

$\boxed{14}$
