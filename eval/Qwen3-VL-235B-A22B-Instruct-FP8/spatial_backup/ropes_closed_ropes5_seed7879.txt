We are shown a scene with multiple intersecting lines of different colors, and we are asked: **“How many closed shapes do the intersecting lines create?”**

---

### Step 1: Understand the question

A “closed shape” is a region completely enclosed by lines — meaning, a bounded area where you can trace the boundary without lifting your pen and return to the start.

We must count **all distinct enclosed regions** formed by the intersections of the lines.

---

### Step 2: Analyze the image

The image at 0:05–0:09 shows a tangle of lines:

- Red
- Yellow
- Cyan
- Purple (dashed)
- Orange

These lines cross each other multiple times, forming a complex web.

We must carefully count **each enclosed region**, not just the number of loops or crossings.

This is similar to counting faces in a planar graph — but here, we are dealing with curves, not necessarily straight lines.

---

### Step 3: Strategy

Since this is a visual puzzle, we must trace the enclosed areas.

Let’s go region by region.

#### Region 1: Top-left small loop formed by red and yellow.

#### Region 2: Small loop near center-left, formed by red and cyan.

#### Region 3: Large oval-like region in center, bounded by yellow, cyan, and orange.

#### Region 4: Small region below center, bounded by red, purple, and orange.

#### Region 5: Region to the right, bounded by yellow, cyan, and purple.

#### Region 6: Small region near bottom-right, bounded by purple and orange.

#### Region 7: Tiny region near top-right, bounded by yellow and cyan.

#### Region 8: Another small region near center, bounded by red and purple.

Wait — this is getting messy. Let’s try a more systematic approach.

---

### Step 4: Use Euler’s formula (optional, for verification)

For planar graphs:  
**V - E + F = 2**  
Where:
- V = vertices (intersections)
- E = edges (line segments between intersections)
- F = faces (regions, including the outer infinite region)

But here, lines are continuous curves, not straight segments, and we don’t have exact counts.

So perhaps better to **visually trace and count**.

---
