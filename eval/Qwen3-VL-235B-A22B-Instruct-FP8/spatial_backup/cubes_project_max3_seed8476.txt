### Step 2: Understand “parallel 2D projection” and “only side faces”

- **Parallel 2D projection** means orthographic projection — no perspective, so no foreshortening. We’re viewing the object from a fixed direction, and we see all faces that are not occluded by other cubes.
- **Only side faces** — this likely means we are **not** counting the top and bottom faces. Only the **front, back, left, right** faces of the cubes are to be considered. (Note: sometimes “side faces” might be ambiguous, but in context, since top/bottom are often excluded in such problems, and the question specifies “side faces”, we assume front/back/left/right.)

So we are to count, from the best possible viewing angle, the maximum number of **front, back, left, or right** square faces that are visible.

### Step 3: Strategy to maximize visible faces

We want to choose a viewing direction that maximizes the number of visible side faces (i.e., not top or bottom).

But since we have multiple cubes, and some may be occluded, we need to find the viewpoint that reveals the most **unoccluded side faces**.
