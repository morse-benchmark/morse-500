We are told to remember color names associated with shapes:

- Red → red triangle
- Orange → orange triangle
- Green → green triangle
- Yellow → yellow circle
- Purple → purple square
- White → white circle

Then we are shown a sequence of movements:

1. First, an arrow moves from the **blue triangle** (blue) to the **green triangle** (green).
2. Then, an arrow moves from the **green triangle** (green) to the **red triangle** (red).
3. Then, an arrow moves from the **red triangle** (red) to the **yellow circle** (yellow).
4. Finally, an arrow moves from the **yellow circle** (yellow) to the **white circle** (white).

The question is: **Which color shape took the shortest amount of time to reach from the previous shape?**

Since we are not given any actual time measurements, we must infer “shortest amount of time” from the **visual trajectory length** — the shorter the arrow path, the less time it likely took (assuming constant speed).

Let’s examine the distances visually:

- **Blue → Green**: The arrow is relatively short — blue triangle is near center-left, green triangle is slightly right and up — close proximity.
- **Green → Red**: Green is near center-right, red is bottom-left — longer diagonal path.
- **Red → Yellow**: Red is bottom-left, yellow is bottom-left but slightly right — very short horizontal move.
- **Yellow → White**: Yellow is bottom-left, white is top-right — very long diagonal.

So comparing the arrow lengths:

- Blue → Green: medium-short
- Green → Red: long
- Red → Yellow: **very short** (they are almost adjacent)
- Yellow → White: very long

Thus, the **shortest trajectory** is from **Red to Yellow**.

But the question asks: **“Which color shape took the shortest amount of time to reach from the previous shape?”**

That is, which *target* shape was reached in the shortest time? So we are to name the **color of the shape that was reached** after the shortest movement.

The shortest movement was from Red to Yellow → so the shape reached is **Yellow**.
