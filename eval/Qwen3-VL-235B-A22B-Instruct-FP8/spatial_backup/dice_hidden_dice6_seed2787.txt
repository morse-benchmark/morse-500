We are shown a sequence of cubes, each with colored faces. First, we are told to remember the color names:

- Blue (light blue square)
- Red (red square)
- White (white square)
- Green (green square)
- Yellow (yellow square)
- Purple (purple square)

Then, we observe 6 cubes arranged in a row. Each cube is a 3D object, so it has 6 faces. However, from the camera’s perspective, only 3 faces are visible on each cube (typically front, top, and one side), meaning 3 faces are hidden per cube.

The question is: **How many red faces are currently hidden from the camera?**

---

### Step 1: Identify which cubes have red faces visible.

Looking at the cubes one by one (from left to right):

1. **Cube 1**: Top = blue, Front = yellow, Side = green → No red visible.
2. **Cube 2**: Top = white, Front = blue, Side = green → No red visible.
3. **Cube 3**: Top = red, Front = blue, Side = purple → **Red is visible on top**.
4. **Cube 4**: Top = white, Front = blue, Side = white → No red visible.
5. **Cube 5**: Top = green, Front = yellow, Side = blue → No red visible.
6. **Cube 6**: Top = purple, Front = blue, Side = red → **Red is visible on side**.

So, red faces are visible on **Cube 3 (top)** and **Cube 6 (side)**.

That means **at least two red faces are visible**.

But the question is about **hidden** red faces.

---

### Step 2: Understand the constraints.

Each cube is a standard cube with 6 faces. We are to assume that each face is painted one of the 6 colors (blue, red, white, green, yellow, purple), and **each color appears exactly once per cube** — since the color key shows 6 distinct colors and there are 6 faces.

This is a standard “colored cube” puzzle assumption unless otherwise stated.

So, **each cube has exactly one red face**.

There are 6 cubes → **Total red faces = 6**.

We saw **2 red faces visible** (on Cube 3 and Cube 6).

Therefore, hidden red faces = Total red faces - Visible red faces = 6 - 2 = **4**.

---

### Step 3: Verify.

Is it possible that a cube has more than one red face? The color key shows 6 distinct colors, and each cube appears to have 6 different colors (e.g., Cube 3: red, blue, purple, and the hidden ones must be green, yellow, white — no repeats). So yes, each cube has exactly one red face.

Thus, 6 cubes × 1 red face = 6 red faces total.

Visible: 2 (Cube 3 top, Cube 6 side).

Hidden: 6 - 2 = 4.

---

### Final Answer:

$\boxed{4}$
