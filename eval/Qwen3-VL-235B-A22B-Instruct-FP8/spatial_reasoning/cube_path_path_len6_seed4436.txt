We are given a cube with colored faces and a path it rolls along. We must determine which color face will be on top when the cube reaches the final red square.

---

### Step 1: Understand the initial orientation

From the video, at 0:07, the cube is shown with:

- Top face: **Red**
- Front face (facing us): **Yellow**
- Right face: **Purple**

We can infer the other faces by standard cube geometry (opposite faces are not adjacent).

Let’s assign the cube’s faces based on initial view:

- Top: Red
- Front: Yellow
- Right: Purple

Then, by deduction:

- Bottom: must be opposite Red → **Green** (since Red and Green are not adjacent in the initial view, and from the color list, Green is the only one not shown on top/front/right)
- Back: opposite Yellow → **Blue**
- Left: opposite Purple → **Orange**

So initial face mapping:

- Top: Red
- Bottom: Green
- Front: Yellow
- Back: Blue
- Right: Purple
- Left: Orange

---

### Step 2: Trace the cube’s path

The cube rolls along a path made of squares. We must simulate the rolling motion step by step.

The path is shown starting at 0:14. The cube starts on the first black square, and rolls along the path to the final red square.

Let’s number the squares in the path:

1. Start position: cube is on first black square (initial orientation as above).
2. Then it rolls **forward** (toward the next square) → this will bring the **front face (Yellow)** down, and the **bottom face (Green)** will come to the front, and the **top face (Red)** will go to the back. But actually, when rolling forward, the top face becomes the back, and the front becomes the bottom.

Wait — let’s be precise.

When a cube rolls **forward** (i.e., rotates 90° around the x-axis, so the front face goes down), the new top face becomes the **old back face**.

But let’s simulate each roll carefully.

### Step 3: Simulate each roll

We’ll track the top face after each roll.

Initial state (on first square):

- Top: Red
- Front: Yellow
- Right: Purple

---

#### Roll 1: Forward to next square (first black square)

Rolling forward → the front face (Yellow) becomes the bottom, the top face (Red) becomes the back, the bottom face (Green) becomes the front, and the back face (Blue) becomes the top.

So after roll 1:

- Top: Blue
- Front: Green
- Right: Purple (unchanged, since rolling forward doesn’t change right/left)

---

#### Roll 2: Forward again (second black square)

Roll forward again → front (Green) goes down, top (Blue) goes back, bottom (Yellow) comes to front, back (Red) comes to top.

Wait — after roll 1, bottom was Yellow (since front was Yellow and it went down).

After roll 1:

- Top: Blue
- Front: Green
- Bottom: Yellow
- Back: Red
- Right: Purple
- Left: Orange

Roll forward again:

- New bottom = old front = Green
- New front = old bottom = Yellow
- New top = old back = Red
- New back = old top = Blue

So after roll 2:

- Top: Red
- Front: Yellow
- Right: Purple

Wait — this is the same as initial orientation!

That can’t be right — we rolled forward twice, so we should be two steps ahead.

Actually, yes — rolling forward twice brings the cube back to original orientation? No, that’s only if you roll in the same direction twice — but in 3D, rolling forward twice doesn’t necessarily bring you back to original orientation unless you’re on a flat plane and rolling in a straight line.

Wait — let’s think differently.

Actually, when you roll a cube forward once, the face that was front becomes bottom, and the face that was top becomes back.

So:

Initial:

- Top: Red
- Front: Yellow
- Bottom: Green
- Back: Blue

After rolling forward:

- The cube rotates so that the front face (Yellow) is now on the bottom.
- The top face (Red) is now on the back.
- The bottom face (Green) is now on the front.
- The back face (Blue) is now on the top.

So new top = Blue

New front = Green

New bottom = Yellow

New back = Red

Right and left unchanged.

Now, roll forward again:

- New bottom = old front = Green
- New front = old bottom = Yellow
- New top = old back = Red
- New back = old top = Blue

So top is now Red again.

Interesting — after two forward rolls, top is back to Red.

But now, the path turns.

Looking at the path at 0:16-0:18:

The path goes:

- Start
- Forward 2 squares (both black)
- Then turns **right** (to the right side)
- Then forward 1 square (black)
- Then turns **right** again
- Then forward 1 square (red)

So total rolls:

1. Forward
2. Forward
3. Right turn (roll right)
4. Forward
5. Right turn (roll right)
6. Forward to red square

We need to simulate each roll.

#### Roll 3: Roll Right (turning right on path)

Now, the cube rolls to the right — meaning it rotates 90° around the y-axis (or z-axis? Let’s think).

When rolling to the right, the right face becomes the bottom, the top becomes the left, the bottom becomes the top, and the left becomes the top? Wait.

Actually, when rolling right:

- The right face goes down
- The top face goes to the left
- The bottom face comes up to the top
- The left face comes to the top? No.

Better way:

Imagine the cube is facing you. Rolling right means it rotates clockwise around the vertical axis? No — rolling right means it tips to the right, so the right face becomes the new bottom.

So:

- New bottom = old right = Purple
- New right = old top = Red
- New top = old left = Orange
- New left = old bottom = Green

Front and back unchanged? Actually, no — when rolling right, the front and back remain front and back.

Wait — let’s define:

After roll right:

- The face that was right is now bottom
- The face that was top is now left
- The face that was bottom is now top
- The face that was left is now right

Front and back remain the same.

So from current state (after roll 2):

- Top: Red
- Front: Yellow
- Right: Purple
- Bottom: Green
- Back: Blue
- Left: Orange

Roll right:

- New bottom = old right = Purple
- New right = old top = Red
- New top = old left = Orange
- New left = old bottom = Green
- Front: Yellow (unchanged)
- Back: Blue (unchanged)

So now:

- Top: Orange
- Front: Yellow
- Right: Red
- Bottom: Purple
- Back: Blue
- Left: Green
