### Step 2: Analyze the nets

All nets are in the “cross” shape: a central square, with one square above, below, left, and right, and one more square attached to one of the sides.

Let’s label the positions for clarity:

In a typical cross net:

```
  T
L C R
  B
```

Where C is center, T=top, B=bottom, L=left, R=right.

And there’s a sixth square attached to one of the sides — in these nets, it’s attached to the right of R, so:

```
  T
L C R S
  B
```

Where S is the sixth square.

Now, when folded, the center square becomes the front face.

The top square (T) becomes the top face.

The bottom square (B) becomes the bottom face.

The left square (L) becomes the left face.

The right square (R) becomes the right face.

The sixth square (S) becomes the back face.

This is a standard folding.
