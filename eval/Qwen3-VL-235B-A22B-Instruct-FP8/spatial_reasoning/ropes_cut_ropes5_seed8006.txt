We are asked: **How many sections does the dotted line cut the colored lines into?**

---

### Step 1: Understand the scene

From the video, we see:
- Several colored lines (white, green, purple, orange, red) drawn in a tangled, overlapping manner.
- A **dotted line** is drawn vertically (or nearly vertically) across the scene, intersecting many of these colored lines.

The question is not asking how many times the dotted line intersects the colored lines, but rather **how many sections (pieces) the colored lines are divided into by the dotted line**.

That is, for each colored line, if the dotted line crosses it, it will split that line into **two sections**. If it crosses a line multiple times, each crossing adds a section.

But note: the question says “the colored lines” — plural — so we must consider **all colored lines together**.

### Step 3: How many sections are created?

Each time a line is intersected by the dotted line, it is divided into **one more section** than the number of intersections.

- If a line is intersected **once**, it becomes **2 sections**.
- If intersected **twice**, it becomes **3 sections**, etc.

So, for each line:

1. White: 1 intersection → 2 sections
2. Green: 1 → 2
3. Purple: 1 → 2
4. Orange: 2 → 3
5. Red: 1 → 2

Total sections = 2 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 = **11**

Wait — but is that correct?

Actually, **each intersection increases the number of sections by 1**.

- A line with 0 intersections → 1 section
- 1 intersection → 2 sections
- 2 intersections → 3 sections
- So, sections = intersections + 1 per line.

Thus:

- White: 1 + 1 = 2
- Green: 1 + 1 = 2
- Purple: 1 + 1 = 2
- Orange: 2 + 1 = 3
- Red: 1 + 1 = 2

Total: 2+2+2+3+2 = **11**

This could be interpreted as: **how many pieces are the colored lines broken into collectively?**

Yes — that’s exactly what we calculated: 11 sections.
