### Step 2: Calculate surface area

Each small cube has 6 faces, each of area 1 unit².

If all 26 cubes were isolated, total surface area = 26 × 6 = 156.

But when cubes are adjacent, they share faces, and those shared faces are **not** part of the external surface area.

So we must subtract the area of the **internal faces** — the faces where two cubes are touching.

### Step 3: Count the number of shared faces

In the 3x3x3 cube with center removed, we need to count how many pairs of cubes are adjacent (i.e., share a face).

We can think of the original 3x3x3 cube (27 cubes) and subtract the shared faces that are lost when we remove the center cube.

So, the remaining 26 cubes form a “shell”.

In a solid 3x3x3 cube, the number of shared faces can be calculated as follows:

- In x-direction: for each y,z, there are 2 shared faces per row (between 3 cubes). There are 3 y and 3 z → 3×3×2 = 18 shared faces in x-direction.
- Similarly, in y-direction: 3 x, 3 z, 2 per column → 3×3×2 = 18.
- In z-direction: 3 x, 3 y, 2 per stack → 3×3×2 = 18.

Total shared faces in solid 3x3x3: 18+18+18 = 54.

Each shared face is counted once (between two cubes), so total internal faces = 54.

Now, when we remove the center cube, we remove all the faces that were shared with it.

- We remove the center cube: it contributed 6 faces to the total (but all were internal, so not part of surface area).
