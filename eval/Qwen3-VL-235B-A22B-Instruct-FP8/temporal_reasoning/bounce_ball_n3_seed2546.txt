We are asked: **“How many seconds did the ball travel after hitting an object?”**

We must analyze the video frames to determine:

1. When the ball first hits an object.
2. How long it continues to travel *after* that first hit.
3. Round the answer to 1 decimal place.

---

### Step 1: Understand the timeline

The video is presented in discrete frames. We assume each frame represents **1 second**, as is common in such problems unless otherwise specified.

Let’s go frame by frame:

- **Frame 0.0s**: Ball (blue circle) on the left, one gray vertical bar on the right.
- **Frame 1.0s**: Ball has moved right, now centered. No object hit yet.
- **Frame 2.0s**: Ball is now touching the right gray bar → **First collision!**

> So, the ball hits an object at **2.0 seconds**.

---

### Step 2: Track movement *after* the first hit

- **Frame 3.0s**: Ball is now moving left, between two bars (one on left, one on right). It has bounced off the right bar.
- **Frame 4.0s**: Ball is now touching the left bar → **Second collision!**
- **Frame 5.0s**: Ball is bouncing back to the right, now between the left bar and two new bars on the right.
- **Frame 6.0s**: Ball is in the middle, moving right.
- **Frame 7.0s**: Ball hits the middle of the two right bars → **Third collision!**
- **Frame 8.0s**: Ball is bouncing left, between the two right bars.
- **Frame 9.0s**: Ball is now touching the left bar again → **Fourth collision!**
- **Frame 10.0s**: Ball is still touching the left bar — appears to be at rest or just finished bouncing.

> The ball continues to move *after* the first hit (at 2.0s) until **Frame 10.0s**, where it appears to stop or settle.

So, from **2.0s to 10.0s**, the ball is still traveling after the first hit.

That’s **10.0 - 2.0 = 8.0 seconds** of travel after hitting an object.

---

### Step 3: Confirm if the ball is still “traveling” at 10.0s

At 10.0s, the ball is touching the left bar. In physics, “traveling after hitting” usually means until it comes to rest or the motion stops. Since at 10.0s it’s still in contact with the bar and not clearly moving further, we may consider that the motion *after* the first hit ends at 10.0s.

Alternatively, if we consider that the ball is still “traveling” *until* the moment it stops moving, and if 10.0s is the last frame where it’s moving (or just finished moving), then we include up to 10.0s.

But note: from 2.0s to 10.0s is 8 seconds of *duration after the hit*.

---

### Final Answer:

The ball traveled for **8.0 seconds** after hitting an object.

$\boxed{8.0}$
